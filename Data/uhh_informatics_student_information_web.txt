## Final Thesis / Degree and Certificate

### General Information

Bachelor and Master Theses

The final thesis completes a course of study and is undertaken in the 6th semester of the Bachelor's program or the 4th semester of the Master's program. However, to write a thesis in a specific area of computer science, it is advisable that you have successfully completed certain introductory as well as subsequent advanced modules in the field. Therefore, it is important to consider early on which area you want to write your thesis in and to select your compulsory modules (especially seminars, projects) as well as, if possible, electives and free electives accordingly. The Department of Computer Science has more than 20 different research areas. Each of these research areas focuses on a specific field of computer science in both research and teaching, and maintains numerous research projects. Look closely at the different module offerings early on and attend an office hour of the faculty if you have questions or would like further information: Research Areas and Research Groups.
Links: https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/inst/groups.html

The local Ethics Committee of the Department of Computer Science at the Faculty of Mathematics, Computer Science, and Natural Sciences at the University of Hamburg issues opinions on the ethical acceptability of research projects involving human subjects as well as research projects with personal data. To determine whether an application is necessary or advisable, please use the basic questionnaire available on the Ethics Committee's website.
Links: https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/home/ethics.html

For a final thesis, you need a topic or assignment, a primary and a secondary examiner, who will evaluate and grade the completed thesis, and if applicable, a supervisor who will guide and assist you while writing the thesis. The specific implementation and assessment criteria for final theses are bindingly defined in the respective study and examination regulations of your program, available under Bachelor Programs and Master Programs. For any questions, please contact the Student Office.
Links: https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/studies/bachelor.html | https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/studies/master.html | https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/studies/orga/stb.html

### How do I find a topic? How do I find examiners?

Each research area consists of a scientific working group in the department, which typically includes at least one professor, usually several scientific staff, doctoral researchers (so-called PostDocs), and other personnel. The supervision of a final thesis is typically the responsibility of a professor from the working group, usually executed by one of the scientific staff members. The responsible professor usually also prepares the primary evaluation. The secondary examiner may or may not be part of the same working group. You can find potential evaluators for your thesis on the evaluators' website.
Links: https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/studies/orga/stud-infos/_boxes/abschlussarbeit_Zeugnis/begutachtung.html

In the case of an external thesis at a company or another scientific institution, the work may also be supervised by a member of that institution. Please inquire about the specific procedures at the Student Office.

The topic selection and supervision are handled differently by each working group. Some working groups publish topics on their homepage and provide specific guidance on who to contact for supervision. Others sit down with the student to collaboratively find a topic or suggest various thematic directions. Some professors expect students to write a short exposé on the topic or solve a brief quiz from the research area. Attending the colloquium, which regularly serves as a platform for presentations of currently conducted final theses, can also generate topic ideas and introduce you to your future examiners.

While the requirements and expectations may vary in detail, you are generally expected to take initiative, inform yourself, and establish contact with the working group. Therefore, closely review the websites of the research areas. If you are interested in a specific topic or wish to write your thesis in that area, contact a staff member from the working group or attend the relevant office hour to discuss further details. You may have several contact discussions with different working groups until you find your topic and supervisor. If a thesis is advertised and the topic fits perfectly immediately, this finding process can be very quick. However, it is not unusual for it to take longer and for you to discuss various possible topics with different supervisors. To determine whether an application to the Ethics Committee of the Department of Computer Science is necessary or advisable, please use the basic questionnaire available on the Ethics Committee’s website. Plan accordingly and allow sufficient time for the preparation of your final thesis. We recommend starting your search at least three months before the planned start of the work.
Links: https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/home/ethics.html

### Evaluation of Final Theses

Status: 20.03.2013 - updated 05.08.2021

### General

With the reform of the examination regulations for Bachelor’s and Master’s programs, the possibility has also been created to expand the pool of potential reviewers for Bachelor’s and Master’s theses. To bring more clarity to the process for students and potential reviewers, the chairs of the examination committees have agreed on the following procedures and possible reviewers.

In the Bachelor's and Master's programs of the Department of Computer Science, the following staff members can take on the review of theses:

a. Professors (including substitute professors and junior professors)
b. Privatdozenten
c. Doctoral staff

A list of the staff of the Department of Computer Science can be found here.
Links: https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/pers.html

Staff members of the Department of Computer Science who are in the doctoral process but have not yet completed their doctorate may take on a review in individual cases if the corresponding head of the work area endorses this in writing and confirms that "the topic of the Bachelor’s or Master’s thesis only relates to the examination material of the courses taught by the reviewers or the corresponding modules" (§ 14 (7) S. 2 PO). In these cases, the final decision is made by the examination committee.

If individuals who do not belong to the above-mentioned groups are to take on the review of Bachelor’s or Master’s theses, a brief explanation of the qualifications of the external reviewer must be included with the thesis application. Supervision by former employees of the Department of Computer Science must also be requested from the examination committee. Reviewers who are not members of the University of Hamburg should have a doctorate. The examination committee makes decisions on justified exceptions.

### Review of the Final Modules in the Bachelor's Programs

The review of final theses in the programs B.Sc. Computer Science, Software Systems Development, Human-Computer Interaction, Computing in Science, and Business Informatics can be performed by the individuals mentioned in No. a.)–c.). At least one of the reviewers must have a doctorate.

For possible reviewers from business administration (B.Sc. Business Informatics) as well as chemistry, physics, biology, and mathematics (B.Sc. Computing in Science), see below.

The examination committee makes decisions on justified exceptions.

### Review of the Final Modules in the Master's Programs

For the initial review of Master’s theses in the programs M.Sc. Computer Science, Business Informatics, Intelligent Adaptive Systems, IT Management and Consulting, and Bioinformatics, a qualification as per No. a.)–b.) is required; for the second review, at least a doctorate is necessary.

For possible reviewers from business administration (M.Sc. Business Informatics), see below.

The examination committee makes decisions on justified exceptions.

For the Master's program in Business Informatics, these regulations were adopted by the examination committee on November 29, 2012, with the restriction that doctoral staff of the Department of Computer Science may only review Master’s theses in M.Sc. Business Informatics whose topic relates to the courses taught by the staff.

### Possible Reviewers for Bachelor’s and Master’s Theses in the Department of Business Administration

The examination committee of the B.Sc. Business Informatics decided on November 29, 2012:

- Professors, privatdozenten, substitute professors, and junior professors of the Department of Business Administration (now: Faculty of Business Administration) may review Bachelor’s theses.

- Doctoral staff of the Department of Business Administration (now: Faculty of Business Administration) who conduct courses in the Bachelor’s program in Business Informatics may review Bachelor’s theses.

- For staff members of the Department of Business Administration (now: Faculty of Business Administration) who are in the doctoral process but have not yet completed their doctorate, regulation b) from the Department of Computer Science (above) applies analogously.

The examination committee of the M.Sc. Business Informatics decided on November 29, 2012:

- Professors, privatdozenten, substitute professors, and junior professors of the Department of Business Administration (now: Faculty of Business Administration) may review Master’s theses.

- The preparation of second assessments may also be carried out by doctoral staff of the Department of Business Administration (now: Faculty of Business Administration) if they conduct courses in the Master’s program in Business Informatics and the topic of the Master’s thesis relates to these courses.

### Possible Reviewers for Bachelor’s Theses from the Departments of Chemistry, Physics, Biology, and Mathematics

The examination committee of the B.Sc. Computing in Science decided on January 21, 2013, that the above-mentioned regulations for the review of Bachelor’s theses in the program Computing in Science should also apply to staff from the other departments involved in the program. In this case, a brief explanation of the qualifications of the reviewer must be included with the thesis application. The examination committee makes decisions on justified exceptions.

### How and Where Do I Register the Thesis?

As soon as you have finalized the topics with the first and second reviewers and any additional supervisors, you need to register the thesis in writing at the Study Office. There is a form that you and each of the first and second reviewers must sign. Please also pay attention to the information in the guideline for the thesis! (See below or in the form center)

After registration, you have five months (for Bachelor’s theses) or six months (for Master’s and Diploma theses) to complete the thesis. Each working group handles this process somewhat differently and has different requirements for the final product (structure, references, formatting, length, language, etc.). You will learn all relevant details from your supervisor.

### What does the colloquium presentation mean?

Colloquium presentation for the thesis

In all Bachelor’s and Master’s degree programs in the department—except for B.Sc. Business Informatics—a colloquium presentation is a mandatory part of the final module. In this presentation, you present the results of your work and answer questions from the reviewers and potential supervisors. The presentation usually takes place after the written work is submitted. However, it is not necessary to wait for the evaluation of the work. In agreement with the reviewers, it is also possible to hold the presentation before the submission deadline of the written work. The scheduling is arranged between students and reviewers.

### What happens after submission and how do I receive my certificate?

The examination office forwards your digitally submitted thesis to the first and second reviewers for evaluation. After receiving both evaluations and the protocol for the colloquium (no colloquium in B.Sc. Business Informatics), the examination office records the grades in your STiNE performance account. If the final module was the last exam requirement in your studies, the examination office produces the final documents (certificate, transcript, Transcript of Records, and Diploma Supplement). No application for the final documents is necessary.

However, please contact the examination office if your performance account still needs to be "organized," meaning, for example, if you have earned too many credit points in one or more areas. In this case, modules or parts of modules can potentially be moved to the Free Electives area or otherwise to the "Additional Achievements" area. (This does not appear on the certificate but on the Transcript of Records of the final documents.) Please let us know as soon as possible where you would like things to be moved!

The preparation of the final documents, including printing at the printing house and the signature by the examination committee chair, may take several weeks. You will be notified by email when the documents are ready, and you can then pick them up or have them sent by post.

Once a semester, there is a graduation celebration in the Department of Computer Science. You will receive the invitation by email.

## Recognition of Achievements

Your fundamental right to recognition of achievements is described in §8 of the MIN examination regulations, which are identical for Bachelor’s and Master’s degree programs:
Links: https://www.uni-hamburg.de/campuscenter/studienorganisation/ordnungen-satzungen/pruefungs-studienordnungen/mathematik-informatik-und-naturwissenschaften.html

(1) Study periods, academic achievements, examination performances, as well as field-related studies or internships that have been completed at a university, an equivalent higher education institution, recognized distance learning programs, or other educational institutions, especially in programs at state or state-recognized vocational academies, or at universities of applied sciences, shall be recognized upon request from the student, provided there are no substantial differences between the acquired knowledge and skills and those to be acquired at the accepting university. Recognition with conditions is possible.

(3) Knowledge and skills acquired in ways other than through study, which are equivalent and required for a successful completion of a study program, can be recognized up to half of the required study and examination performances.

Thus, here is how to submit an application for the recognition of achievements:

- At the beginning, your personal decision on which modules should be recognized stands. The basis for this is, in accordance with the legal foundations cited above, that comparable competencies exist between modules of your previous studies or studies abroad and the modules or areas of the current program. If needed, your subject advisor is available for consultation during office hours or at a personally arranged appointment. Note: In the B.Sc./M.Sc. Business Informatics program, applications for recognition of achievements must also be submitted for language courses at the University of Hamburg (as so-called "extracurricular achievements") if they are to be included in the free elective area according to the decisions of the examination committees. In other degree programs with a free elective area, language courses can be credited through the Department of Computer Science by sending proof of course completion to the study office (studienbuero.inf"AT"uni-hamburg.de) via email without having to submit an application.
Links: https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/studies/orga/stb.html

- You fill out the recognition application in STiNE. Detailed information, including a step-by-step guide for submitting the STiNE application, can be found in these slides. Don't forget to virtually "submit" it after the application is stored in STiNE.
Links: https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/studies/orga/forms/anrechnung-leistungen-infovortrag.pdf

- Inform the study office (studienbuero.inf"AT"uni-hamburg.de) via email or during office hours that your application has been submitted. Provide your application number and your (new) degree program. You will receive a summary of your application as a PDF file, which you must print and sign; a directly "drawn" signature on the document (if you have a suitable tool for this) is also possible. This document serves as a template for the decision of the chair of the examination committee.

- The following documents must be submitted digitally (scan/readable photo, unless already in digital format): Signed summary of your application that you received from us, Transcript of Records, certificate, or other evidence of the achievements to be credited. This is not necessary for achievements already recorded in STiNE at the University of Hamburg. Module descriptions. Exception: No module descriptions are needed for modules that are identical in the new degree program, or if a learning agreement has already confirmed the recognition of a module in advance (in which case, send the learning agreement instead).

- Special case: In the B.Sc./M.Sc. Business Informatics program, a copy of the high school diploma (or other university entrance qualifications) is also required for language courses, according to the decision of the examination committees. This applies to language courses at the University of Hamburg for which an application for recognition of an extracurricular achievement is necessary if they are to be included in the free elective area.

- You will receive a written notification regarding the decision of the chair of the examination committee.

There is no specific deadline for submitting the application; however, note that a module or a module component cannot be recognized if you have already officially entered the examination procedure (i.e., examinations have already been conducted or a future examination is registered and the withdrawal deadline has passed). Nevertheless, we recommend timely submission of applications after the start of your studies or immediately after earning the achievement (e.g., right after returning from the semester abroad).

If there are uncertainties: The subject advising office is available to assist you with all questions concerning the recognition of achievements.

### ERASMUS+

The ERASMUS+ program of the European Union offers students the opportunity to spend one or two semesters abroad at a European partner university. ERASMUS+ provides many advantages: exemption from tuition fees at the host university, financial support through a monthly allowance, and administrative assistance. Every year, over 500 students from the University of Hamburg take this opportunity to broaden their horizons and gain international experience. In addition to the study stays program line, internships in the EU area are also supported. Each institute or department has its own network of ERASMUS partners, which is managed by program coordinators. Points of contact for interested students are the ERASMUS offices in the faculties and departments.
Links: https://www.uni-hamburg.de/internationales/studierende/outgoing/austausch-gaststudium/erasmus/im-ausland-studieren.html

### Learning Agreement

The conclusion of a learning agreement is recommended for every study-related stay abroad. It contains a study plan, i.e., a list of the modules to be taken at the host university, and secures, provided approval has been granted by the responsible examination committee, prior recognition of the modules taken. This enables the student to reliably plan that successfully completed modules abroad can be credited towards their studies. For an exchange through ERASMUS+, a completed learning agreement is mandatory and thus part of the application documents. For stays abroad that are organized outside formal exchange programs, a learning agreement can be voluntarily concluded, for which the Department of Computer Science provides a form.
Links: https://www.uni-hamburg.de/internationales/studierende/outgoing/austausch-gaststudium/erasmus/im-ausland-studieren.html | https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/studies/orga/forms.html

Links: https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/home/ethics.html

## Evaluation of Teaching

In the Department of Computer Science, a teaching evaluation is conducted every semester to ensure and improve the quality of teaching, involving a survey of the students. Individual courses are evaluated, not modules.

The surveys are conducted using online questionnaires, the access link to which is distributed in the courses after about two-thirds of the lecture period. After being completed by the students, the online questionnaires are processed technically by the TEAM Evaluation. The evaluation results are sent as soon as possible to the respective instructor via email, enabling a discussion of the feedback with students during the ongoing lecture period. The survey uses various types of questionnaires (one for lectures and exercises, one for seminars/pre-seminars, one for projects, one for internships). The questionnaires can be filled out in German or English to enable international students to participate in the teaching evaluation.

## Maternity Protection

As an expectant mother, you enjoy special protection during pregnancy and after childbirth. Since January 1, 2018, female students are included in the Maternity Protection Act (MuSchG), as it now encompasses training relationships where the training location, time, and process of training events are mandated, which applies to studies at the University of Hamburg.

The maternity protection period typically begins six weeks before the expected due date and ends eight weeks after childbirth. During this protection period, the following particularly applies:

- In normal cases, a prohibition on participating in courses...

- ...and if the student explicitly wishes to be exempted from the prohibition on participating in courses, an exemption from the attendance requirement in courses for examinations and breastfeeding periods.

- Suspension of all deadlines according to the examination regulations, particularly the corresponding extension of the deadline for final theses.

## Disability Compensation

According to §11(1) of the Bachelor and Master of Science examination regulations, students with disabilities or long-term or chronic illnesses are entitled to compensation for disadvantages, provided that the disability or illness results in a disadvantage in the performance of examination and study achievements:
Links: https://www.uni-hamburg.de/campuscenter/studienorganisation/ordnungen-satzungen/pruefungs-studienordnungen/mathematik-informatik-und-naturwissenschaften.html

If a student credibly demonstrates that they are unable to fulfill examination achievements partly or entirely under the prescribed conditions due to a disability or long-lasting severe or chronic illness or to take the exams within the deadlines specified in these regulations, the chair of the examination board may, upon written request, take appropriate compensatory measures. (…) The same applies to study achievements.

Decisions regarding such compensatory measures must be made on an individual basis, as they can vary depending on the type of disability or illness and according to the examinations and study achievements specified in the module.

In the case of exam assessments, such measures could include:

- Extension of the processing time for an exam

- Allowance for interruptions during exam processing

- Conducting the exam in a separate room

- Allowance for personal or technical assistance (e.g., laptop with dictation software)

- Change of the examination format

According to §11(2) of the Bachelor/Master of Science regulations, it is necessary to obtain the recommendation from the coordinator for the concerns of students with disabilities or chronic illnesses for compensation for disadvantages. Once you have received this recommendation, apply for the approval of the compensatory measure at the Computer Science Student Office. The student office will inform you once the chair of the examination board has decided on your application.
Links: https://www.uni-hamburg.de/studieren-mit-behinderung.html

After your application has been approved by the chair of the examination board, you must contact the organizers of the relevant module in good time before the exam and request the provision of the compensatory measure. Take care to apply in good time: Consider that it may not be feasible to implement your compensation measure organizationally very shortly before the exam, especially if additional rooms or supervisors are needed.

## Participation, Attendance Requirement, and Inactivation (e.g., Exercises)

As a general rule - and many studies support this: Attendance is the key to success in passing a module.

In exercises, seminars, internships, projects, and other courses, except for lectures, attendance is generally required. In such events, according to §9(2) of the MIN Examination Regulations B.Sc./M.Sc., active participation is conditional on missing no more than 15% of the sessions. Under normal circumstances for a course with 13-14 weekly sessions during the lecture period of the semester, this means that you will be classified as inactive by the organizers if you miss more than two sessions. If there are fewer sessions with mandatory attendance (e.g., a block seminar that is compressed into two full days over a weekend), usually missing just one session will lead to classification as inactive. Active participation is also not fulfilled if you have not completed the study requirements defined in the module description (e.g., obtaining 50% of the points from the assignments in an exercise).

The consequence of being classified as inactive is that you will not be able to complete the module provisionally, and no credit points can be earned: For successful completion of the module with credit point acquisition, you need at least one instance of active participation in each course of the module—for which, in “pure” lectures (without integrated exercises, etc.), just registration suffices, as classification as inactive is excluded. If you wish to continue completing the module (which is mandatory in the case of a compulsory module), you must participate again in the event in one of the following semesters without being classified as inactive.

Active participation in later semesters cannot be "overwritten": For example, if you have actively participated in an exercise but failed the exam for the same module and participate again in the exercise the following year for repeat purposes, your active participation will not be revoked even if you are classified as inactive this time.

To check whether you have actively participated in an event from previous semesters, proceed as follows: After logging into STiNE, click on "Studies" ("Studium") in the navigation bar at the top, then select "Partial achievements" ("Teilleistungen," under "Examinations"/"Prüfungen") in the sidebar. Choose the relevant semester from the dropdown box in the main window and then click on the "Examinations" ("Prüfungen") link next to the module in question. In the popup window that appears, a green checkmark will appear next to events with active participation. Note that STiNE defaults to assuming active participation after registering for a current or future event, and typically inactive classification is compiled at the end of the semester, meaning for events in the current or upcoming semester, the presence of the green checkmark does not yet confirm that you have been active. If you know that you will not be able to attend an event with alternative offerings in each semester (e.g., proseminar, seminar, internship) and the withdrawal deadline (which usually runs until Thursday in the second week of lectures in a semester) has passed, you may ask the organizers to preemptively classify you as inactive, allowing you to choose an alternative event in time for the next semester's registration phase—because as long as you are still registered as an active participant, registration for an alternative event is not possible.
Links: https://www.stine.uni-hamburg.de/

Also, note that there is no limit on attempts for active participation in events (unlike with exams), meaning you can repeat participation in an event each time you have been classified as inactive.
Links: https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/studies/orga/stud-infos/_boxes/pruefungen.html

If you are not responsible for violating attendance requirements in a course, e.g., due to illness, the organizers have the option, according to §9(2) of the MIN Examination Regulations B.Sc./M.Sc., to waive the inactive classification in exchange for fulfilling an additional requirement defined by them (e.g., completing an extra assignment). However, this is a voluntary concession on the part of the organizers: You have no entitlement to such a requirement. Please understand, especially in "large events," that organizers may find it unfeasible, due to capacity constraints, to create and evaluate such requirements individually.

In the case of a disability or chronic illness that makes adhering to the normally required attendance obligations or study achievements disproportionately difficult for you, an adjustment of conditions in the context of disadvantage compensation may be considered.
Links: https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/studies/orga/stud-infos/_boxes/nachteilsausgleich.html

## Part-Time Study

If there is a valid reason (e.g., employment of at least 15 hours/week, childcare, disability, chronic illness) that allows you to devote at least half but not the full working hours to your studies, you may apply for part-time status. A part-time study may be granted on request for two consecutive semesters, and repeated applications are permissible. In a part-time study, the deadlines and schedules of the university examination regulations are typically extended such that one academic semester corresponds to two university semesters. The semester fee must be paid in full. Applications are made via STiNE. Pay attention to the application deadlines, which must be completed in advance: Part-time for a summer semester must be applied for by January 31, and for a winter semester by June 30. More information can be found on the CampusCenter website.

## Transcript of Records

The Transcript of Records (also known as "Transcript" or "ToR") is an academic record issued by the university that lists your degree program, previously completed modules with grades and earned credit points, and your current average grade. Such a document can be requested by potential employers or other universities.

If you, as a student in the Department of Computer Science, need a Transcript of Records, please contact the Student Office of Computer Science by email (studienbuero.inf"AT"uni-hamburg.de). Please provide the following information in your email:

- Your full name, program of study, and student ID number

- Form of the Transcript of Records: Digital (i.e., PDF file with a validation link) or paper

- Language of the Transcript of Records: German, English, or both

We will provide you with the Transcript of Records as a digital document in your STiNE account or notify you when the paper version can be picked up at the Student Office of Computer Science during office hours or by appointment.
Links: https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/studies/orga/_boxes/office-hours.html

Note that you do not need a Transcript of Records when applying for a Master's program in the Department of Computer Science at the University of Hamburg. Instead, you can submit a printout of your STiNE performance account and the confirmation of registration for the bachelor's thesis, if already completed.

## Leave of Absence

Students who cannot dedicate at least half of their working hours to their studies for important reasons may apply for a leave of absence. Important reasons include illness, childcare, or a stay abroad. Leave semesters do not count as subject semesters. A leave of absence generally excludes the acquisition of study and examination credits (exceptions are exam retakes and exams during study abroad). The application is made via STiNE. Please note the deadlines for submitting your application, which must be done in advance: Leave for a summer semester must be applied for by January 31, and for a winter semester by June 30. First-semester students may only be granted leave in exceptional cases. Further information can be found on the CampusCenter pages.
Links: https://www.uni-hamburg.de/campuscenter/studienorganisation/studienverlauf/beurlaubung.html
